## 3. Research: what a club site has to do

Sources: UX case studies of club directories (UVA, Northeastern capstone, Cal Poly UX Fest SLO), club-site guides, and existing BSA sites (UofT, UTM, York, UBC). No formal survey of students exists; these are informal.

1. **Say what the club is in one screen.** Sites that fail this lose visitors (Cal Poly redesign). → Hero: name, one line, who it's for, "free".
2. **Join and "what's next" visible without scrolling.** → Join button in the nav on every page; next event directly under the hero.
3. **Most traffic comes from the Instagram bio on a phone.** The real competitor is the Linktree. → Mobile first; the site does the Linktree's job (join, newsletter, perform, socials) plus what Linktree can't (event details, add to calendar, archive).
4. **Say up front if joining needs approval or a login.** → Join card says Rubric needs an @mylaurier.ca login.
5. **Other BSA sites are directory listings or blogs with constitution / exec / members pages.** None show a record of events. → An events archive by term is the cheapest proof the club is active and lasting, without stating a founding year.

## 5. Anti-slop rules applied

- No generic hero line, no "vibrant community", no three icon cards. Every section points at a real event, date or link.
- No gradients, glows, glass, stock photos or emoji. One motif (the disc) used three ways: hero sun, event date, numbered steps.
- Copy is plain and short. Unknown fields are left empty, not filled.
- Contrast checked: dark text on the red disc (4.5:1); white fails there (4.0:1).

## 6. Built

Next.js 16 static export, Tailwind 4, Inter + Noto Sans Bengali. Pages: `/`, `/events/`, `/about/`, `/join/`, `/contact/`, 404. Per-event `.ics` files, sitemap, robots, Open Graph card, Organization and Event structured data. Upcoming vs past is decided in the browser in Toronto time, so nothing needs a daily rebuild. Detail: `bsa-website/README.md`.
